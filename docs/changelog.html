Changelog

Release notes are published alongside each build on GitHub Releases. This page summarises them.

v1.4.0 — 2026-10-12 current

The last public release was v1.2.2; v1.3.1 was prepared and never published, so this release carries both halves. The headline additions are Halo 3, Halo 2 Anniversary editing and Halo Reach Theater.

Theater — Reach saved films

  • Saved films open and play. File ▸ Open theater clip… loads a Reach .mov (including the in-progress .temp recording of a match you are still playing), draws the film's own Forge map, and plays the recording back with transport controls, a scrub timeline (View ▸ Theater timeline), free / follow / over-the-shoulder / first-person cameras and a cast list. See Theater.
  • Players are the characters they really were. Each body is drawn as the biped the recording names — Spartans and Elites — skinned and posed by the game's own animation graphs, wearing the armour, the armour effect and the colours that player had chosen, and holding the weapon the recording gives them (including the weapon the gametype's loadout spawns them with).
  • Movement from the recording, not guessed. Walk, run, back-off and strafe cycles are driven by measured stride rates per weapon class; idle clips play at the engine's 30 fps (they used to run nearly eight times too fast); players ride, enter and leave vehicles; melee swings, weapon fire, reloads and assassinations are read off the film's own event channel rather than inferred from how a body moved.
  • Deaths. A killed player plays the ragdoll clip their own character has (the Elite's is named differently, which is why Elites used to stay standing), falls to the floor under the gravity measured from the recordings, and lies where they fell until the recording itself removes them — several seconds, as in game — instead of vanishing after five.
  • First person. Looking through a player's eyes draws their own arms and the weapon in them, both posed by that weapon's own first-person animation, with the player's forearm and chest pieces on the hands. Each species has its own arms.
  • The game's own HUD. The player list is built from Reach's own scoreboard widget tags, and floating player names use Reach's own font, team-coloured, with no background plate — both read from the game's assets rather than drawn by us.
  • Statistics. Theater ▸ Statistics and --film-stats report per-player time, distance, weapons, vehicles, deaths and the match score out of the recording, and name who was responsible for about nine deaths in ten.
  • What a recording does not contain is now said plainly rather than silently approximated: a Reach film records only the recorder's own kill feed, most medals and assists are absent, one body per clip has a live look direction, and a clip can hold more players than it holds gamertags.

Halo 3

  • Maps open in the viewer — textured clusters and instances, baked lightmaps, skies, fog, scenario objects and object lighting from the map's light probes.
  • Map variants open in the editor with the map's Forge palette, attachments, water and damage states, and save byte-for-byte against every shipped variant.
  • You can place objects. Dragging a palette piece into the viewport, the Line and Fill tools and the place verb all used to refuse silently unless a variant was already open; a shipped variant is now adopted as a blank canvas.
  • Lighting is no longer about a fifth too dark. Halo 3 was being put through Halo Reach's tone curve; it now uses its own (1.5c − 0.5c³), which lifts everything below white by about 22%.

Halo 2 Anniversary

  • Map variants can now be saved. H2A was read-only. Its variants now open, edit and save through the full Halo 4 editor, and HMS reproduces 283 of the 289 shipped H2A variants byte-for-byte (the six are duplicates of two files whose own stored checksum matches no formula over their payload — MCC never checks it).
  • All seven maps surveyed and fixed: skies, ocean tint, layered material families, fog and the fallback sky gradient.

Halo 4

  • Exposure and bloom follow the engine chain, Forge lightmap burn shadows, object lighting from surface and air probes, an exposure override in the GUI, the grid family, a faster sky and fog.

Editing

  • Prefabs. Save a selection as a reusable building block (.hmsprefab) and drop it into any map, in any supported game. Tools ▸ Prefabs.
  • Every control can be rebound. Settings ▸ Controls & keyboard lists all 38 editor actions, with left-handed presets; flight keys bind by physical position, so AZERTY and Dvorak layouts work.
  • Convert a map variant to another game. File ▸ Convert variant to another game… re-creates a Forge map on another title's canvas. All four titles work as a source and as a target, and every piece that cannot be re-created is reported with the reason rather than quietly left out.
  • Save precision preview. A ghost of the pose a save can actually store, plus snap-to-representable — rotation is the big loss (a Halo 3 facing angle is eight bits).
  • Damage states. The Object panel shows an object's damage state (minor … destroyed); kept in the project, never written to the .mvar.
  • Spawn camera and physics settle. The camera opens on the variant's spawn points; placed objects can be settled onto the ground with the map's collision.
  • Model import. Imported geometry keeps its materials, is selectable and movable like any other object, and is browsed through the same file picker as everything else.
  • Open recent lists what you last opened — variants, base maps, projects and theater clips.

Rendering

  • Forge pieces have their reflections, and the shine nothing asked for is gone. Every Forge object was shaded with the material constants of its first part, so a reflective catwalk was matte and a plain platform had a sheen the map never authored. Each part now carries its own specular, roughness and environment map, read from the object's own materials.
  • The energy sword has its two glowing blades (five additive layers, three of which were being discarded), and energy blades and vehicle accent lights no longer have their red and blue exchanged.
  • Parked Forge vehicles no longer glow with lit headlights, brake lights and dashboards.
  • Weapons and equipment lying in a map are the size the game draws them — about a third larger than before — and a held weapon is the size the weapon tag states for that player's species.
  • Solid barriers no longer draw see-through, and switching a damage state no longer leaves other states untextured.
  • Forge objects appear much faster instead of trickling in one at a time; Theater playback on a busy 16-player clip is several times faster and no longer grows in memory as it plays.

Other

  • Modded and Workshop maps. Maps installed from the Steam Workshop no longer load with missing geometry: the parser could not find MCC's stock shared.map (it only looked beside the map), so every model paged out of the shared cache failed to decode. Variants saved against a Workshop map are routed to the right base map by its MCC map GUID instead of the -2 placeholder the file carries. Load time for a large modded map dropped from 47 s to under 8 s.
  • Graphics & performance settings. A Settings page that trades picture quality for speed: presets, render scale, shadow / post / texture / draw-distance knobs, a frame cap and an idle throttle.
  • Auto-update (opt-in). The app checks GitHub Releases once a day and shows a small Update available pill next to Help; the Update window downloads the archive, verifies it, installs it after you close the app and restores your session (see Updating). Settings ▸ Updates turns the check off.
  • Linux. Maps are found on udisks-mounted drives and in the Xbox app's layout, not only under Steam; settings and projects use XDG paths instead of a Windows-only environment variable.
  • Version numbers: the app, the archives (HaloMapStudio-1.4.0-<platform>.zip plus .sha256) and these pages now share one version.

v1.1 – v1.2 — 2026-09-16 / 2026-09-22

Released as the v1.1.x pre-releases and v1.2.1 / v1.2.2 on GitHub; the list below is what they added over v0.1.0.

  • Variant properties. Every global field of a map variant (Reach and Halo 4) is now decoded and shown in a Variant properties group of the left panel - ids, stamps, activity / mode / engine, category, budget, flags, the world bounds, the chunk length / SHA-1 / _fsm and the per-piece quota table with palette names - with the engine's field names on hover; category, maximum budget, world bounds (behind a warning) and quota min / max are editable; variant get / variant set do the same from scripts. Reach saves now write a correct mvar chunk length and SHA-1 and mirror header edits into the chdr chunk (both were left stale before). See Variant properties.
  • Halo 4 Forge editing. A Halo 4 map variant now opens as an editable scene: select / box-select with the outline, G / R, gizmo, Shift+D, Delete, undo / redo, drag-and-drop placement from the map's own Forge palette (localised names, the game's placement defaults per piece), the Object panel with the Halo 4 record (quota / variant, team and colour, object type, spawn order / time, four labels, parent, placement bits, shape in world units, type extras, a raw (unverified) group and an editor-only scale), mass edits, and place / set / get / save as in scripts. File ▸ Save writes the open .mvar in place and Save As writes a new one, both through the engine's own encoder after a save check (objects outside the variant bounds are clamped, stale types are reported; the budget and the palette maxima are not checked, because the game ignores both). The encoder reproduces every shipped variant byte-for-byte and an HMS-saved variant loads in MCC. See Halo 4.
  • Halo 2 Anniversary (early, view-only). MCC's groundhog caches load through the same pure-Rust reader as Halo 4 (the format is identical apart from one pointer constant): the map picker gains a Halo 2 Anniversary group, and the maps render with their geometry, textures, baked lightmaps and post chain. Map variants are not yet decoded, so it is view-only for now.
  • Forge special FX. Placed special-FX objects (Colorblind, Gloomy, Juicy, Nova, Olde Timey, Pen and Ink, Dusk, Eerie, Golden Hour) now apply their screen effect, composed like the game. Settings ▸ Forge special FX (persisted), script screenfx on|off|get, headless HMS_FORGE_FX=0.
  • SCALED / SHADOW pseudo-flags. Per-object checkboxes in the Object panel with (auto) / (set) state (right-click resets to automatic), stored in the project rather than the .mvar; [scaled] / [shadow] markers in the objects list; global switches Scaled objects and Shadow casters (Forge) in Settings (persisted). Scripts: set … scaled|shadow on|off|default, scale, shadowcasters, flags; headless HMS_SCALED, HMS_SHADOWCASTERS.
  • Objects outside playable space. Objects inside a BSP flagged "not normally playable space in multiplayer" get an orange ! in the objects list and a warning strip in the Object panel; Save / Save As first show a window listing them (double-click a row to select and fly there; Save anyway). Script bspwarn list.
  • Soft ceilings (map floor). View ▸ Soft ceilings (map floor) draws the invisible planes that bound a Reach map's playable space — the floor under Forge World, the outer walls and the sky ceiling — as a translucent fill plus edges that show through the terrain and water (red = soft kill, orange = acceleration, yellow = slip surface). Off by default, persisted (soft_ceilings.txt); script softceilings on|off|get (get lists every ceiling); headless HMS_SOFT_CEILINGS=1. Trigger volumes moved from Settings to the View menu (script triggers on|off|get).
  • Hard floor (world bounds). View ▸ Hard floor (world bounds) draws the map's world box — the physics broadphase the game builds from every structure BSP's bounds (sky and hidden BSPs included) plus 64 units, the invisible wall nothing can pass — as a magenta box with its floor gridded at the lowest height (Forge World: -75, where you stop with the soft ceiling off). View ▸ Playable BSP bounds draws the smaller per-BSP boxes (violet). Both off by default, persisted (hard_floor.txt, playable_bounds.txt); scripts hardfloor, playablebounds; headless HMS_HARD_FLOOR=1, HMS_PLAYABLE_BOUNDS=1.
  • View menu overlays. Show map spawn points (the base map's own spawn markers, off by default) and Show hidden-block physics hulls (the variant's invisible blocks, on by default; never map objects or level geometry), both persisted; scripts mapspawns and outlines; headless HMS_MAP_SPAWNS, HMS_PHYSICS_OUTLINES.
  • Teams. The team drop-down lists neutral (no team) first and none (unset) last; new objects start on the neutral team, as in the game. Script set … team accepts names (red … pink, neutral, none).
  • Hidden blocks are real objects. Click the hull to select an invisible block, box-select takes every block whose hull overlaps, the selection wireframe is the hull, and magnet / face mates / array duplicate use the hull extents so copies tile edge to edge.
  • Scripting. New verbs select box, undo, redo (GUI), outlines, mapspawns, screenfx, scale, shadowcasters, flags, bspwarn; the console's built-in quick reference now lists every verb.
  • Camera and preview. The cursor is hidden and captured while flying with the right mouse button and while moving / rotating objects (locked on Windows and Wayland, confined on X11). The palette's 3D preview renders through the viewport's object path, with the map's lighting.
  • Rendering. Engine-exact Forge lights and power-ups, armor-ability pickups, vehicles with their attachments, the Tree of Life and foliage, water categories, decals and per-object lighting.
  • Settings. Fly speed and the camera stand-off live in Settings ▸ Camera, render & display; diagnostics under Advanced. Settings files: forge_fx.txt, scaled_objects.txt, shadow_casters.txt, map_spawns.txt, physics_outlines.txt.
  • Earlier: left panel reorganisation, searchable variant object list, placement tools moved to the Tools menu, Edit and View menus, Help ▸ Keyboard shortcuts as the shortcut reference.

v0.1.0 — 2026-09

First public binary release (Windows x64 and Linux x64 archives). Source code is planned to follow once the renderer and the Forge editing workflow settle.

  • Halo: Reach map viewer with engine-faithful lighting, materials, water, sky, fog and effect particles; automatic discovery of Steam and Workshop maps.
  • Forge variant (.mvar) open / edit / save, including Forge properties, labels, parents, boundaries and the 651-slot budget.
  • Blender-style editing (grab / rotate / duplicate, axis locks, magnet, precision, array), gizmos, box select, mass edit, undo/redo, Line and Fill placement tools.
  • Construction (CAD) tools: guides, circles, squares, face-to-face mate, anchor, mirror, fill edge.
  • Forge script language with an in-app console, headless --script / --exec runner, a local command server and the hms-mcp MCP bridge.
  • Headless PNG rendering (HMS_SHOT), --dump-maps and variant inspection flags.
  • Optional real-time probe GI and a path-traced lighting bake.
  • Halo 4 support: engine-faithful rendering and Forge variant editing (open / edit / save), read with a pure-Rust cache parser.
  • Projects (.mmsproj) and a quick-save session slot; settings stored per user.
